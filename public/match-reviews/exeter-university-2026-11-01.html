In Her Own Words

Thurrock T-Birds v Exeter University

Sunday 1 November 2026 · Women’s Championship · Home

Thurrock T-Birds 29–45 Exeter University

Result — Loss Full 80 minutes Won all 16 own scrums Two against the head

From the day — Thurrock T-Birds v Exeter University at Oakfield

Match overview

Thurrock went into this game with just 15 players available, meaning everyone had to play the full 80 minutes. Exeter had 22 players and were able to make full use of their replacements.

We also found ourselves down to 14 players on several occasions, which made the challenge even harder.

Considering the circumstances, a final score of Exeter 45–29 Thurrock wasn’t a bad result. We had some good periods of play and created several opportunities to score, but unfortunately made mistakes at crucial moments which prevented us from finishing them off.

Personal performance

Personally, I don’t feel I had a good game today. In fact, I would probably say it was my worst performance for quite a while.

I wasn’t really mentally switched on, and I think that affected most areas of my game. My statistics don’t necessarily look terrible, but I came away feeling disappointed with my overall contribution.

I don’t want to make excuses, but my boots were causing me serious problems. They had already been rubbing my heels during previous games, and by the end of this match I was bleeding through my socks. It was uncomfortable and distracting, although I still feel I should have performed better.

My tackling is normally one of the strongest parts of my game, but today it just wasn’t there. I made six tackles and missed two. One player stepped me, while the other came from mistiming my approach and trying to grab rather than properly complete the tackle.

My carries were also disappointing. A lot of the passes were coming behind me, meaning I couldn’t build momentum before contact. I struggled to make the gains I would normally expect from myself.

Positives

Despite feeling I had a poor game, there were some positives.

Scrums

Probably my strongest area today.

  • Won all 16 of my own scrums.
  • Won two against the head.
  • No scrum losses.

Lineouts

  • Won 9
  • Lost 2

Rucks

  • Won 16
  • Lost 0

Try-scoring opportunities

I got over the line twice but unfortunately didn’t come away with a try.

  • First occasion: I was high tackled and we were awarded a penalty close to the line.
  • Second occasion: I was held up over the line.

I also came very close to scoring on another occasion, finishing just a few feet short before we managed to regain possession.

It’s frustrating not to have converted those opportunities, but it shows I was still getting myself into attacking positions.

Personal assessment

Personal rating: 3/10

This is one of those games where the numbers don’t tell the whole story.

On paper, winning every scrum, including two against the head, winning 16 rucks and getting over the line twice are positives.

However, I judge myself on more than just statistics. I know what I’m capable of, and I didn’t feel I brought my usual intensity, concentration or physical presence today.

The biggest disappointment was my tackling. I normally pride myself on being reliable defensively, and missing two tackles out of eight attempts isn’t what I expect of myself.

I also need to work on carrying into contact when the pass isn’t perfect, rather than allowing poor delivery to affect my momentum.

Things to work on

  • Mental preparation: Making sure I’m switched on from the first whistle.
  • Tackling: Getting my timing and technique back to where they should be.
  • Carrying: Learning to adjust to passes behind me and still generate forward momentum.
  • Boots: Getting the heel problem sorted before the next game. I can’t afford another 80 minutes of playing in pain.
  • Consistency: Making sure one poor performance doesn’t become two.

My overall thoughts

I don’t want to dress this performance up as something it wasn’t. I wasn’t happy with my contribution, and I know I can do much better.

There were positives, particularly in the scrum, but I expect more from myself.

We competed against an Exeter side with seven more players available, and despite having no replacements and spending periods with 14 players, we still scored 29 points.

Personally, I need to put this one behind me, take the lessons from it and make sure I’m better next week.

One bad game doesn’t define me, but how I respond to it matters.
